# Indonesia / Wax & Copper — brief frozen before authoring

## Research and interpretation
UNESCO's Indonesian Batik inscription (2009), https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/indonesian-batik-00170, describes dots and lines of hot wax resisting dye, with repeated dyeing/wax removal. UNESCO's educational overview https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/32618-EN.pdf distinguishes batik tulis from copper-stamp batik cap. Retrieved search excerpts establish this craft connection; no photographs or traditional motif templates are copied.

The design translates resist lines into ivory-on-indigo textile-like skins and a low copper openwork ornament. The original looping petal-and-dot motif is invented for this asset, NOT an authenticated named batik pattern or a representation of all Indonesian communities. The copper ornament is inspired by the material of stamps, not a functioning stamp. No sacred or status-specific symbols or inscriptions.
